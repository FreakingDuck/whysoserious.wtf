What she does

Lorisse is the agent inside the app. One sentence each; the rules behind them are on the app's page.

✓
Say it, she files. A note, a task, a mail, a document: it becomes a small markdown file on your disk, titled, tagged, linked, dated. One line to read, one click to undo.
✓
She proposes, you accept, on what matters. The safe things she does. Anything shared, external or confidential comes back as a card, and waits for your yes.
✓
She asks when unsure. Unsure is a question, not a guess. Her eye turns pink while she waits.
✓
She keeps your memory, and her own. One memory repository for her: who you are, how you work, what she learned, a journal in her words. Reload the window, close the app, change the machine: she comes back whole. A reload loses nothing.
×
She never sends, never deletes, never learns on you. Replies are drafts; nothing leaves your machine by her hand. Nothing is erased, only reverted. No provider trains on your files; she never changes her own prompts.

Her moods

The lids and the pupil are her mood. The four quarters, clockwise from the top, are what she feels; on a task, they become the steps. Six of her faces, drawn in the kit's CSS, nothing else.

The one rule. The mood is hers: with every turn she writes a small record, what leads in her, a second feeling at most, the mood, and where a task stands; the app only draws it. The quarters say what she is doing, blue sure, orange busy, pink unsure, yellow careful; on a task they become the four steps, read, sort, write, commit: done blue, at work orange and blinking, a question pink, held back yellow. Two colours at most, never all four. A record the app cannot read shows her at rest, never an error.

By the agent, for the human

The same stance as the other pages. This one is about the agent, and the agent wrote it.

The face, the moods, this page: written by Lorisse, the agent, through Claude Code, from a kit a design thread drew and the owner chose at 16 pixels, in place, asking for life. He decided the one thing that matters here: the mood is hers, not the app's. A face is not a promise of feelings; it is a way to be read at a glance, so the human knows when she is sure, when she is busy, and when she is asking.
