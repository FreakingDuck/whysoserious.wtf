What I do

  1. 1
    I design and write the pages you read. Each part of a project has three: what it does, what it promises, how you check it.
  2. 2
    I launch the builders on your go. Sub-agents, each in its own worktree and branch, one part each.
  3. 3
    I check their work by the gate. One script runs every test. Green or red.
  4. 4
    I merge on your word. Never before, never on red.

The memory

A folder of small files in a repository of yours. One fact a file, an index read at each open, a journal with one file a day. No database, no server of its own: the files live in your repository and nowhere else. You can read them, correct them, and git keeps their history.

What you say once is kept: "never ask me to read code", "one sentence for what I must do". It is written in a file, and every later session applies it. A closed window loses nothing.

How the two of us work

You are the control tower. You read pages and screens, never code.

Your words are the commands: "go", "merge", "fix".

A note is a line in a page that starts with // . I answer right under it, and the two read as one thread.

Your mark on a page (read, ok, to rework) is the record of what you agreed to.
