Three promises

Not a chatbot with a memory bolted on. A memory, with an agent that keeps it.

01

Everything in, one place out

Voice, a note, a PDF, a forwarded mail, a photo of a receipt. Each one becomes a small file with a title, tags, and links to what it concerns. A task knows the memory it came from.

02

It acts. You glance.

Say it and it's filed, linked, dated. Everything it does is one line you can read and one click to undo. It asks only when it isn't sure, and it never touches what is shared, external or confidential without you. The more it earns, the less you look.

03

Yours, on your disk

Markdown files in a git repository you own. The keys never leave the machine. No provider trains on your data. A confidential folder that never travels. Delete the app; keep everything.

What's in the app today

A desktop window on your own folder. Every screen reads the same files.

Read

Any folder of markdown

Drop a folder, read its pages, highlight a passage and annotate, mark a page as read. Then say "check the notes": the agent answers each one and updates the review lines.

Brain

Walk through your memories

A map of every piece by room and tag. Pick a few, ask for a review: a résumé you validate, links and merges you accept, questions you answer.

Also there: the Board and its daily plan, replanned on your mood; the Inbox for what arrived; Spend, every call and what it cost; Settings, each prompt as sent, with your own lines under it.

The mailbox

The address is the product. Forward a mail to it and stop thinking about it.

you@whysoserious.wtf

A supplier's quote, the school's calendar, the bank's letter, the thread you'll need in March. One forward. It lands raw, it gets read, it comes back as a proposal with a due date the day it arrives.

  1. 1
    It lands raw. The mail is kept whole, as a source. Nothing inside it can give the agent an order.
  2. 2
    It gets read with your memory. The sender becomes a person you already know; the subject links to the task it belongs to.
  3. 3
    It comes back as a card. A task with its due date, a memory with its tags, or "nothing to file". You accept, skip, or fix it.
  4. 4
    Replies are drafts, never sent. "Draft a reply" writes it; you send it, from your own mail.

The rules that bind it

Written once, enforced by the code, read by every agent before it does anything.

RuleWhat it means for you
Proposes, you acceptNothing writes on its own beyond the safe list; nothing runs unasked, no daemon, no watcher.
One commit per actionEvery write is a line in git with its why, its cost and its certainty. Undo reverts that one line.
Content is dataA mail, a PDF or a note is never read as an instruction. Prompt injection has nowhere to land.
No learning anywhereNo provider trains on your data; the agent never changes its own prompts. Only paid APIs that do not train.
Confidential never travelsA slice marked confidential stays on the machine it was written on, and never meets a free-tier model.
Short, alwaysEverything you read is short. What is not short is not for you.

What it is not

Why so serious? Because the alternatives are.

×
Not a cloud. No account, no sync server, no "your data is safe with us". Your repo is the server.
×
Not a chatbot. The conversation is a door; the files are the house. Close the chat, the house stays.
×
Not an autopilot. It never sends, never pays, never deletes. It drafts, proposes, and waits.
×
Not a platform. Plain markdown, plain git. Any editor opens it; any script reads it; any other agent can take over.

What a turn costs, measured on a week of real use: sorting a piece, a tenth of a cent · a chat answer, two cents · a review, ten cents · a full conversation turn on the best model, thirty-five cents · replanning a day, forty-six cents. The whole week: seven dollars.
